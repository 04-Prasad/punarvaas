★Our Six Objectives

Each objective maps directly to what SIH judges score. This is what we are building, and why.

🎯
Objective 1 — Identify Red Zones at Sub-Village Level

Go beyond whole-village labelling. Use H3 hexagonal grids (~120 m) to pinpoint exactly which hamlets within a village are unsafe — so we can say "420 of 600 residents" instead of "this village is risky".

Judges: Problem Understanding + Innovation
🏔️
Objective 2 — Auto-Discover Safe Relocation Sites

Don't just accept uploaded candidate sites — automatically find, rank and explain the best relocation options within a livelihood-preserving radius, with hard legal exclusions and suitability scoring.

Judges: Technical Depth + Feasibility
💧
Objective 3 — Compute Carrying Capacity by Binding Constraint

A site's true capacity = its scarcest resource (usually water). Compute per-resource limits against real Indian norms (JJM, RTE, IPHS), name the bottleneck, and cost the gap in ₹ mapped to the government scheme that pays.

Judges: Technical Depth + Impact
📋
Objective 4 — Produce a Budgeted, Phased Relocation Plan

Rank habitations into Immediate / Short-term / Medium-term / Mitigate tiers. Solve who moves where, in which year, under a real budget — with benefit–cost analysis and consent as a hard constraint.

Judges: Innovation + Impact
🔍
Objective 5 — Make Every Decision Fully Explainable

Clicking any Red Zone answers 8 questions: which hazard, which datasets, what resolution, what factors, how confident, who approved the threshold, when last updated. Zero unexplained colours.

Judges: Technical Depth + Q&A Survival
✅
Objective 6 — Prove It on Real Disasters

Feed the system only pre-event data and check if it would have raised the alarm. Wayanad 2024, Joshimath 2023, coastal erosion in Odisha — validated backwards, not just claimed forwards.

Judges: Presentation + Impact
The single strategic bet Every team will show a red map. We will show a signed, costed, phased government decision — and prove it would have worked on a disaster that already happened.

01What the Problem Statement Actually Asks

"Intelligent Identification of Hazard-Based Red Zones, Carrying Capacity Assessment, and Immediate Relocation Needs for Vulnerable Habitations."

The PS opens with a story: a village of 1,200 residents below a landslide-prone slope. The government already knows it's vulnerable. What it cannot answer is ten questions — which we group into three engines:

RISK Engine 1 — Where is it unsafe?
  1. Is the entire village unsafe, or only some parts?
  2. Which hazards affect it?
  3. How severe and frequent are those hazards?
  4. Should the population be relocated immediately?
  5. Permanently, or only seasonally?
SITE Engine 2 & 3 — Where can they go?
  1. Where can residents be relocated?
  2. Is the proposed site itself safe from other hazards?
  3. How many people can the new site support?
  4. Does it have water, roads, hospitals, schools?
  5. Which villages get priority when resources are limited?
The sentence that defines the whole build The PS says today this data "may exist in separate maps, reports, spreadsheets and government departments. There is no unified intelligent system that converts it into a clear relocation decision."

Our product is that conversion layer. Everything we build must end in an action, a number, and a rupee figure.

Three non-negotiables from the PS

❶ A decision, not data

Weak: "This village is high-risk."

Strong: "420 of 600 residents face unacceptable risk. Site B holds 300 due to water. Relocate 110 highest-risk households first."

❷ Not a black box

AI in 8 named slots only. Land law, water norms, zone approval → must be rule-based, expert-controlled.

❸ Fully explainable

Click any Red Zone → see which hazard, which datasets, what factors, who approved, when last updated.

02The Core Insight That Wins

Go sub-village The PS's very first question is "Is the entire village unsafe or only some parts?" Every competing team answers at village-centroid level — one dot, one colour, one verdict. We answer at hamlet and building level. That single choice lets us say "420 of 600 residents" instead of "this village is risky".
EVERYONE ELSE Village-level
// one row per village
village_id: "IN-UK-CH-0417"
risk:       HIGH
population: 1200
verdict:    "relocate"

Implies moving all 1,200 people. Costs 4× more than needed. Government shelves the file.

PUNARVAAS Hamlet + building level
// ~120 m H3 hexagons inside the village
hex ...e1  RED     pop 198  hh 41
hex ...e3  RED     pop 222  hh 47
hex ...f7  ORANGE  pop 104  hh 22
hex ...a2  YELLOW  pop 380  hh 79
────────────────────────────
RED total  420 of 1200 · 88 households

Move 88 households, not 250. Cost drops ~65%. The file gets approved.

How: Dasymetric Population Disaggregation

Census gives population per village. We redistribute it onto building footprints, weighted by area × estimated storeys — so every H3 hexagon has a defensible population count.

Honesty is the strategy This is an estimate and we label it as one. Every disaggregated figure carries a confidence score, and the field-verification app can replace it with a surveyed household roster. The PS forbids claiming "software replaces field surveys" — so we make the survey a first-class feature.

03The Three Chained Engines

The PS names three questions. Each is an engine, and each feeds the next. The chain is the product.

Why chaining matters A village is only "Immediate relocation" if a viable destination exists with enough capacity. Otherwise the honest verdict is "Immediate risk, no viable site — build site capacity first" — a completely different government action with a different budget line. Systems that don't chain can't tell those two apart. Ours can.

04Engine 1 — Risk Scoring

Combine multi-hazard maps → score exposure and vulnerability → classify into 4 zones → explain every number.

The risk formula

Multi-hazard union (not a weighted average)
H = 1 − Πi (1 − hi)
A weighted average lets one catastrophic hazard look "medium". A probabilistic union will not.
Risk, expressed two ways
Rscore = 100 · Ĥ × Ê × V̂  |  EAP = Σ pi·popi·fi
R (0–100) for ranking. EAL in ₹ for benefit–cost analysis — which is what unlocks government money.

Four zones — rules, not colours

RED Unacceptable risk. → Relocation or urgent intervention.
ORANGE High risk requiring inspection. → Short-term mitigation and monitoring.
YELLOW Moderate risk. → Preparedness and building restrictions.
GREEN Comparatively safer. → Possible relocation candidate site.

Zones are assigned by a versioned YAML rule engine — editable by an SDMA, carrying an approving authority + version. Thresholds are policy, not engineering.

05Engine 2 — Safe-Site Discovery

We exceed the PS here The PS only asks for site upload. We also auto-discover and rank candidates — because the officer who doesn't know where to move people is the officer who needs this tool most.

⌾Hindcast Validation — Our Credibility Weapon

Feed the system only data available before a known event. Check whether it would have raised the alarm.

🏔️ Wayanad Landslide (Jul 2024)

Feed: Only pre-2024 hazard, terrain, rainfall data

Expect: Mundakkai–Chooralmala hamlets classify RED, landslide governing

🏚️ Joshimath Subsidence (Jan 2023)

Feed: InSAR velocity up to Dec 2022

Expect: Rule R-01 fires on sustained creep → RED before cracks appeared

🌊 Coastal Erosion, Odisha

Feed: Shoreline positions 1990–2015 only

Expect: Erosion-rate projection condemns the settlement inside the 10-year line

✅ Resettlement site match

Feed: Run Engine 2 blind around an eroded village

Expect: Our top-ranked candidate matches the site the state actually built

Rules for doing this honestly Hard data cutoff. No post-event imagery. Model trained outside the test district. If a test fails, report it and explain why. A team showing one honest failure is more credible than a team claiming six perfect hits.

☀Real-World Impact

1 · Live unmet backlog

Uttarakhand, Himachal, Sikkim, Assam, Odisha — hundreds of villages queued for rehabilitation with no systematic prioritisation tool. Officers rank villages on files and site visits today.

2 · Makes relocation fundable

BCR + scheme-convergence output converts "this village is unsafe" into a financeable DPR annexure with scheme codes and ₹ figures.

3 · Makes relocation legitimate

Provenance + audit + versioned thresholds + consent + grievance register = decisions that survive an RTI request, a PIL, and a village meeting.

4 · Reduces failed relocations

Scoring livelihood continuity and social disruption stops the pattern of half-empty colonies and families walking back.

5 · Creates a public good

Open, versioned Habitation Risk Registry keyed to LGD codes — reusable for damage assessment, land-use regulation, insurance design.

6 · Fills a documented global gap

The PS states it: no country has combined all five (risk + sites + capacity + phasing + explainability) in one system. We do.

The line to end the pitch on "Every rupee spent moving the wrong village is a rupee not spent saving the right one. PUNARVAAS is how a Collector proves which is which — and can show his working."

⚠Guardrails — 7 Claims We Must Never Make

The PS dedicates a whole section to this. Violating it in the demo is an instant loss.

Forbidden claimWhat we say instead
✕ "AI will automatically relocate people." AI ranks and explains. Humans decide. Relocation requires DDMA + SDMA approval.
✕ "This site is legally approved." "Legally screened against available layers; statutory clearance is separate."
✕ "The model can order forced displacement." "No household is scheduled without recorded consent." Consent is a hard constraint in the optimiser.
✕ "Every Red Zone is permanently uninhabitable." "Unacceptable risk under current conditions — mitigation can de-escalate."
✕ "100% accuracy." "AUC 0.89, confidence scores shown, known limitations documented."
✕ "Software replaces field surveys." "It targets field surveys where they change decisions most."
✕ "Satellite data determines capacity." "Capacity needs CGWB groundwater, facility records and ground verification."
Turn this into a slide "What our system deliberately does not claim." It reads as maturity and pre-empts the hardest Q&A questions.
